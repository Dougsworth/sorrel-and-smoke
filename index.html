A note from the people tending the fires.

Christmas in Kingston smells like two things.

The first is sorrel. Somewhere around the second week of December, every kitchen on the island seems to have a pot of those deep red petals steeping with ginger, pimento and a few cloves, waiting for the sugar and the rum. The second is smoke. Pimento wood smoke, drifting from oil drum grills on roadsides from Boston Bay to Half Way Tree, carrying jerk pork and chicken and a little bit of everybody's patience with it.

Sorrel & Smoke puts both of those smells in one garden for one long Saturday. Forty cooks, a dozen rum makers, a demo kitchen under the old poinciana trees and a band on the bandstand. You come hungry in the morning, you leave full and a little sunburnt at night.

We built the day around the people who actually make this food. Pitmasters who have been lighting the same drum since the eighties. A fisherman who cooks what he caught before sunrise. An Ital kitchen that has never once opened a can. There are no celebrity stages here, just good cooks and enough shade to sit and eat what they make.

And at sunset, when the light goes gold over the Blue Mountains, the whole garden stops for a moment and raises a glass of this year's sorrel. That is the bit people tell us they come back for.

    How the day goes

    Twelve hours, from the first coal to the last record. Demos are free with any pass. Seats at the rum tasting come with the Tasting pass.

    1. 10:00

      Gates open

      The jerk pits have been lit since before dawn, so the smoke is already there to meet you. Blue Mountain coffee at the gate while you get your bearings.

      Old Hope Road gate

    2. 11:00

      A slow walk down Jerk Pit Row

      Odessa Brammer takes a small group from drum to drum, talking pimento wood, marinade and how long is long enough. Tasting as you go.

      Jerk Pit Row

    3. 12:30

      Chef demo: escovitch, done properly

      Kemar Lyttle fries a whole snapper and builds the pickle of scotch bonnet, carrot and onion in front of you. Bring questions. He likes questions.

      Demo kitchen under the poincianas

    4. 14:00

      Ital kitchen: rundown from the coconut up

      Imani Tafari grates and squeezes fresh coconut, then boils it down slowly with pumpkin, green banana and callaloo until it turns to custard.

      Ital Grove

    5. 15:30

      Rum tasting: five glasses, aged to overproof

      The Overproof Library pours five Jamaican rums side by side, from a gentle eight year old to the white rum your uncle keeps for emergencies. Seated, for adults only.

      Rum Tent, Tasting pass holders

    6. 16:45

      Live on the bandstand: The Hope River Players

      Banjo, rhumba box and a horn section. Mento, ska and old Christmas songs played the way your grandparents danced to them.

      The bandstand

    7. 17:40

      The sorrel toast at sunset

      The band stops, the garden goes quiet and everyone raises a glass of this year's first sorrel as the sun drops behind the hills. Your Day pass includes the glass.

      Sunset Lawn

    8. 18:30

      Kingfish Hi Fi till ten

      A sound system on the lawn playing rocksteady, early dancehall and lovers rock until the gates close. The jerk pits stay open to the end.

      The bandstand lawn

    The cooks

    Three of the people tending the fires this year, in their own words.

    Jerk is not a sauce. It is a fire you keep company with for six hours.

    Odessa Brammer

    Pitmaster at Boston Smoke Yard, Portland. Thirty one years on the same oil drum. Leads the walk down Jerk Pit Row at eleven.

    If the fish was swimming yesterday, I do not need to do very much to it today.

    Kemar Lyttle

    Fisherman and cook at Old Harbour Catch. Goes out at four, cooks by ten. Runs the escovitch demo at half past twelve.

    Ital food is just Jamaican food that remembers where it came from.

    Imani Tafari

    Cook and farmer behind Ital Garden Kitchen in St Thomas. Everything she serves grew within a day's drive. Teaches coconut rundown at two.

      By shuttle

      Free buses run every twenty minutes from Liguanea and Papine Square, from nine in the morning till half past ten at night.

      By car

      Limited parking inside the gardens at J$1,000 for the day. It fills by noon, so the shuttle is the better bet.

      By taxi

      Drop off and pick up at the Old Hope Road gate. There is a marked taxi line after dark.
